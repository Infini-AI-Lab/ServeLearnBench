How well can agents self-improve from serving experience?

Haizhong Zheng1, Yizhuo Di1, Ranajoy Sadhukhan1, Shuowei Jin2, Beidi Chen1

1Carnegie Mellon University  ·  2Amazon

Deployed agents work under policies, constraints and preferences that are partly undisclosed and change over time. In ServeLearnBench an agent serves a stream of tasks whose hidden policy shifts between windows. After each task it sees only a score, and held-out tests at the end of every window measure what it actually learned.

Benchmark Design

How ServeLearnBench works

Agents serve evolving task streams in Retail, Banking, and Pitch. Across three difficulty tiers, they must learn new environment knowledge, revise it as conditions change, and handle returns to earlier states.

Findings

What the evaluation shows

Capability is not learning

95.4vs 14.1

Mean Hidden score with the policy disclosed (Oracle) and without it (Blind). Every model can do the tasks once told the policy, yet on Retail L3 the best of 28 learning pairs reaches only 59.6 (median 22.0).

Adaptation is costly

4–101× Oracle's cost

Inferring a hidden policy costs 4 to 101 times as much as following a disclosed one (Kimi K3: Prime $2,479 vs. Oracle $102), and it can degrade behavior that needed no learning.

Exploration is the bottleneck

ρ = 1.00

A method's answer diversity while it is still failing ranks the five methods exactly as their Hidden reward does (ρ = 0.75 over 30 method–domain–tier cells).

Learning can be fast after first success

78% / 76%

Once Prime and CH find the correct behavior, they quickly reuse it, averaging 78% and 76% correctness over the next five encounters. For these harnesses, finding the right behavior is a larger obstacle than reusing it.

Analysis

How the scores come about

How reliably do agents learn, what does learning cost, and which behaviors distinguish effective learning harnesses?

Methods that explore more tend to learn better

The five harnesses have exactly the same ranking by exploration and Hidden reward (ρ = 1.00). Prime and CH try more distinct answers, while weaker learners often keep repeating the same behavior.

A large learning gap remains

Across the four open-weight models, Prime and CH achieve average Hidden scores of 61.7 and 57.6, ahead of the other learners at 38.3–43.4. Yet even the strongest learner remains far below Oracle's 94.5.

Higher reward comes at a substantial cost

Prime achieves 59.0 Hidden reward at $0.195 per task. CH reaches 55.1 at $0.083, delivering nearly as much reward at less than half the cost.

Learning speed depends strongly on the harness

Prime and CH learn new policies fastest in both domains. After five examples of a Banking policy, both answer roughly 60% of subsequent tasks correctly; progress is slower in Retail.

Learning can damage already-correct behavior

On Fully Specified Retail tasks, every learning harness falls below Blind's 98.9% accuracy, reaching only 89.5–95.5%. This loss is not universal: Prime and Mem0 improve on Blind in Banking.
